# SlavicGame

> A 3D Adventure RPG / Action RPG inspired by Slavic mythology, folklore, medieval life, and supernatural cosmology.

## Vision

SlavicGame is a large-scale, multi-year RPG project focused on exploration, combat, monster hunting, magic, alchemy, rituals, gods, folklore, player choice, and a living world.

The game is not intended to be a historical reconstruction. It is a fictional fantasy world inspired by Slavic cultures and the realities of roughly the 9th–10th centuries, combined with supernatural mythology.

Core themes:
- mystery and the unknown
- life and death
- freedom versus destiny
- family and identity
- faith, nature, power, betrayal, and responsibility.

## World & Setting

The game takes place in its own fictional world rather than historical Poland or another existing country. It has its own kingdoms, tribes, cultures, cities, villages, religions, conflicts, geography, and history.

Several Slavic-inspired cultures coexist. They may differ in clothing, architecture, customs, warfare, art, music, dialects, and religious traditions. Non-Slavic-inspired cultures also exist.

Historical inspiration includes architecture, weapons, clothing, crafts, agriculture, trade, settlements, burial customs, rituals, and everyday life. Historical material is used as inspiration rather than as a claim of exact reconstruction.

## Jawia, Nawia and the Divine Realm

```text
                 ????
                  |
           something older
             than gods
                  |
          +-------+-------+
          |               |
      DIVINE REALM      NAWIA
          |               |
          +-------+-------+
                  |
                 JAWIA
            world of living
```

- **Jawia** — the world of the living.
- **Nawia** — the world of the dead.
- **Divine Realm** — a layer in which gods may exist or manifest.
- **Fourth Sphere** — something older than the gods and one of the central mysteries of the setting.

The boundary between Jawia and Nawia is beginning to break. Spirits, monsters, anomalies, returning dead, changing locations, and other supernatural phenomena appear. This is initially understood as the main crisis, but later becomes a symptom of something deeper.

## Historical & Cultural Foundation

The project uses a research-first approach. Every major mythological or historical element should be classified according to its evidence and documented separately from the game's fictional interpretation.

Research chain:

**Historical evidence -> Scholarly interpretation -> Regional tradition -> SlavicGame interpretation -> Original fantasy**

## Pantheon Bible

The pantheon is intended to contain a broad catalogue of Slavic divine figures rather than only Perun and Veles.

Important research rules:
- distinguish well-attested figures from regional, late, disputed, and reconstructed material;
- document the region and tradition associated with each figure;
- document what is known, what is interpreted by scholars, and what is uncertain;
- clearly mark the point where SlavicGame invents new material;
- gods are not automatically good or evil.

### Evidence Classification

|---|---|
| A | Strongly attested |
| B | Attested / ambiguous |
| C | Regional |
| D | Late / disputed |
| E | Reconstruction |
| F | Original SlavicGame material |

### Gods and Player Choice

Gods have individual goals, values, followers, interests, and conflicts. The player may gain divine favor, become a follower, choose a patron, potentially maintain relationships with multiple divine powers, reject the gods, complete divine quests, receive blessings, and use divine magic.

Divine relationships can also be damaged or betrayed and may have serious consequences. Gods may speak, appear, influence the world, send servants, make deals, help, deceive, or fight. Some may become bosses. Whether gods can truly die is a major unresolved mystery.

## Player Character

The player creates a custom character who begins as a monster hunter. The character initially appears ordinary and is not presented as a chosen one.

Over time, the player discovers that their identity and family history are connected to the deeper mystery of the world.

Planned features:
- Slavic-inspired name selection;
- nicknames, titles, and reputation-based epithets;
- unknown family history at the beginning;
- family history as a major part of the main story;
- player-driven development rather than a predetermined heroic identity.

## Magic System

Magic can originate from gods, nature, souls, Nawia, ancient power, and the Fourth Sphere.

Forms of magic include:
- spells;
- symbols and rune-like signs;
- rituals;
- alchemy;
- herbs;
- divine magic;
- Nawia magic;
- ancient magic.

The game will contain an original magical alphabet and language. Signs can combine into words, symbols, spells, and rituals.

Powerful magic has costs such as energy, health, ingredients, relationships with gods or Nawia, effects on nature, reputation, or physical transformation.

## Combat

Combat combines melee weapons, bows and other ranged attacks, and magic.

Planned systems include blocking, dodging, attack direction, distance, stamina, armor, weak points, status effects, and magical effects.

Weapons include swords, axes, spears, knives, maces, bows, magical weapons, divine artifacts, and monster-crafted weapons.

## Creatures & Bestiary

The bestiary draws from Slavic folklore while allowing original fantasy creatures.

Examples include Leszy/Leshy, Rusałki/Rusalka, Wodniki/Vodnik-type beings, Utopce, Południce, Strzygi, Zmory/Mory, Kikimory, Mamuny, Boginki, Domowiki, and many others.

Not every supernatural being is hostile. Some can be neutral or friendly, persuaded, contracted, tamed, used as companions, or encountered as guardians.

## Companions & Relationships

The game will contain multiple potential companions. Companions may join, leave, die, betray the player, or remain loyal depending on the player's actions.

Optional romance can be tied to decisions, trust, and relationships.

## Living World

The world is not designed as one enormous empty map. It is divided into large, detailed regions such as:

1. starting region
2. great forest
3. marshes
4. mountains
5. ruins
6. major settlements
7. Nawia
8. divine realms
9. additional regions added during development.

### Day and Night

Daytime supports trade, work, travel, hunting, exploration, and normal settlement life.

Night changes the world rather than merely darkening it. Monsters, ghosts, anomalies, altered behavior, fog, and supernatural events become more prominent.

### Weather

Weather includes rain, snow, fog, storms, drought, and wind. Weather can affect gameplay and the environment.

Thunderstorms may sometimes be connected to Perun, but a storm is not automatically evidence of Perun's presence.

### Settlements and NPCs

Villages are intended to function dynamically. Settlements may grow, decline, lose or gain inhabitants, develop trade, and change their available quests.

NPCs have roles, relationships, schedules, work, and daily routines. The long-term goal is a world that continues to function when the player is not watching.

### Reputation

Reputation is tracked separately for villages, regions, factions, cultures, gods, and individual NPCs.

### Festivals and Rituals

Festivals and rituals use real Slavic motifs as inspiration: fire, water, ancestors, seasons, funerals, protection, and agricultural cycles.

Historical evidence must be distinguished from fantasy interpretation.

## Decisions and Endings

Player decisions can affect NPCs, villages, reputation, gods, companions, quests, the state of the world, and the ending.

There will be multiple endings rather than a simple good/evil split. The final outcome should depend on the player's accumulated history and relationships.

## Death

Player death is not permanently final. The player returns to a checkpoint.

Future development may introduce consequences connected to repeatedly crossing the boundary between life and death.

## Engine & Technical Architecture

The project is written in **C#** and uses a custom engine/framework architecture with **Veldrid** as the primary graphics abstraction. The initial runtime uses Veldrid's SDL2 windowing layer and requires a Vulkan-capable GPU/driver for the graphical runtime. CPU-only regression checks do not require a window or Vulkan.

## Research Principles

SlavicGame treats mythology as a research subject as well as a source of inspiration.

Documentation should explicitly separate:
- primary/medieval evidence;
- later folklore;
- scholarly interpretation;
- regional tradition;
- reconstruction;
- original SlavicGame fiction.

The pantheon is therefore a living research document. New figures, sources, interpretations, and regional traditions can be added without presenting uncertain material as historical fact.

## Research References

Initial research should prioritize academic and specialist sources on Slavic religion and medieval Slavic cultures, including Cambridge scholarship and source-critical work on Kievan Rus and Polabian traditions.

Important reference areas include scholarship on the limited and indirect nature of evidence for pre-Christian Slavic religion, Vladimir's Kievan pantheon, and regional traditions from Polabia and Rügen.

## Project Philosophy

SlavicGame is a multi-year project. Development should proceed through small, testable systems and vertical slices rather than attempting to build the entire world at once.

The central design goal is a **living world**, not a checklist of locations.

The intended player feeling is:

> I really lived in this world.

## Build and run

Install the .NET 10 SDK and a Vulkan-capable graphics driver. Linux also requires SDL2 (`libSDL2-2.0.so.0`).

```sh
dotnet build SlavicGame.csproj --configuration Release
dotnet run --project SlavicGame.csproj
dotnet run --project tests/SlavicGame.RegressionTests.csproj --configuration Release
```

Controls: WASD move, left Shift run, mouse rotate camera, F11 toggle fullscreen, Escape exit.

Current runtime: procedural terrain, third-person orbit camera, terrain following, region detection, day/night clock and FPS HUD. The other systems above are planned.
